Introduction

A brief background to the writing tradition at the center of this project.

The Fengxian Brocade Belt Script is a female-exclusive writing system native to Jinhui Town in the Fengxian District of Shanghai, traditionally woven directly into ribbons. Integrating Oracle Bone script, Chu Seal script, and Clerical script characters with pictographic patterns, it is a logographic system. It exists as an entirely independent framework from the phonetic Nushu (Women's Script) of Jiangyong, Hunan. Passed down from mothers to daughters or replicated among women, these ribbons are typically woven with colored cotton threads onto brocade. They are most commonly featured on wedding gowns and traditionally used in pairs.

Working with the Script

I moved between visual comparison, reference materials, tentative interpretation, and interviews. The process became less about “solving” the script and more about testing what different forms of evidence could actually support.

Comparing forms before interpreting meaning

While looking for materials on brocade-belt writing and women’s scripts, I came across Nüshu reference materials and began comparing the forms side by side. Some characters appeared visually related, but similar-looking shapes did not necessarily carry the same meaning. That difference made me more cautious about treating resemblance as proof of a shared reading.

Reading three woven belts

To make the interpretive process more concrete, I selected three belts from the collection and added short English renderings based on the woven text. These translations are presented as careful, research-based readings rather than fixed final meanings.

Brocade belt

Translated lines

“Auspicious blooms herald days of bliss, as you voice a chant for the mountain orchids.”

“Beholding five sister-orchids in their prime, one hastens home, bearing the day’s fresh catch.”

Brocade belt

Translated lines

“Under a golden sun, the fisherman reaps his harvest, as blessings of orchids line the narrow lane.”

“Returning home, a daughter spies plum blossoms glowing in the warmth, and gently steals a kiss from the beautiful petals.”

BROCADE BELT

Translated lines

“On this day of heavy snow, my heart yearns for my dear Jialang. I pray he is blessed with good fortune, and keeps himself warm in thick robes.”

“Sitting alone, Jiaoniang recalls so much of days gone by, longing only for her lord to return home one day, clad in silks of gold and glory.”

The interview changed the question

I brought this question to an older woman who still wove the belts: did the characters carry any meaning for her? She told me she simply thought the patterns were beautiful and had learned by watching her neighbor weave ribbons. Her answer changed the question for me. I began wondering how a tradition could continue even after parts of its original meaning had faded—through patterns, craftsmanship, imitation, and memory.

Selected Language Research

These two papers examine second-language acquisition from complementary directions: how digital and multimodal input is designed, and how learners internally reorganize a new language through interlanguage.

2026

The Influence of Interlanguage on Language Understanding and Cognitive Processes in Second Language Acquisition

Jiaxin Wan · Sole author · Journal of Education, Humanities and Social Sciences · IMPES 2025 · Vol. 62 (2026), pp. 228–233

Research focus

How does interlanguage—the learner-built system between a first language and a target language—shape comprehension and longer-term cognitive processing for Chinese learners of English?

Approach

A literature-based analytical study of Chinese–English bilingualism, organized around pronunciation, word order, pragmatics, and the internalization of language patterns.

Key argument

Interlanguage is not random error. It is a structured, dynamic system, but native-language transfer and fossilized patterns can create persistent difficulties in phonology, syntax, pragmatic meaning, and culturally dependent interpretation.

Examples examined

The paper discusses consonant-cluster pronunciation, English inversion and interrogative word order, pragmatic transfer in expressions such as “you should…,” and difficulties with irony, idioms, metaphor, and spatial frames.

Teaching implications

Teachers should diagnose the source of interlanguage patterns, strengthen listening and speaking through recording and digital resources, teach pragmatic and cultural cognition explicitly, and create sustained spaces for L2 practice.

What interested me

The project pushed me to think of language learning as cognitive restructuring: learners do not simply replace one set of words with another, but gradually build and revise an internal linguistic system.

Multimodal Approaches to Second Language Acquisition in the Digital Age

Zhengyuan Bian, Hanqi Lu & Jiaxin Wan · Co-author and corresponding author · Authors contributed equally · SHS Web of Conferences 220, 04025 (2025)

Research focus

How can digital learning materials combine language, image, sound, and interaction to make second-language learning more accessible, engaging, and efficient?

Approach

The study used observation and multimodal discourse analysis of Crash Course: Linguistics, examining how verbal explanation, visual structure, sound, and interaction work together. Resource selection was also informed by a review of popular-science and English-teaching videos on YouTube.

Key finding

Coordinated multimodal cues can make abstract linguistic concepts easier to follow and increase immersion and interest. Short, vivid, interactive digital resources can support learning when their modes reinforce rather than compete with one another.

Digital-age tension

The paper also identifies limits: technology dependence, the digital divide, content-quality control, and the difficulty of evaluating whether digital engagement actually produces durable learning.

Recommendation

Rather than replacing face-to-face learning, the study recommends a blended model that combines offline interpersonal interaction with online tools, supported by multidimensional assessment of language development and intercultural communicative competence.

Publication

Published in the ICLRC 2025 proceedings as an open-access paper in SHS Web of Conferences.

How the two projects connect

Taken together, the papers let me look at second-language acquisition from both sides of the learning process: the external design of input and the internal system a learner constructs while interpreting it. That connection is what led me toward questions at the intersection of language, cognition, and learning.
