Fengxian Brocade-Belt Script

I first encountered this local women’s writing tradition in a chronicle of Fengxian, where I grew up. What began as a question about how to read the woven symbols became a broader inquiry into how language, craft, memory, and imitation can carry a tradition forward even when parts of its original meaning fade.

Independent field research · Grade 10–Present

From character comparison to cultural transmission

I compared surviving woven forms with a published character index, traced recurring symbols and variants, organized audio, visual, and text records, and spoke with local practitioners about how patterns were learned and transmitted.

Fieldwork · archival comparison · local interviews · public documentation · exhibition
Research focus

Working with incomplete evidence

Some woven forms aligned with indexed characters; others varied, repeated, or resisted direct interpretation. I learned to treat the index as a starting point rather than a final answer.

Research shift

When meaning becomes pattern

Local weavers explained that some forms were learned and repeated for their beauty even when meanings were no longer known. That shifted my question from “Can I decode everything?” to “How can a tradition survive partial loss?”
